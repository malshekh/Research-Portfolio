# TDP-43 Domain Architecture
## Overview
TDP-43 is a 414-amino-acid RNA/DNA-binding protein composed of four major functional regions. These domains regulate RNA binding, protein oligomerization, nucleocytoplasmic transport, and protein-protein interactions. Dysfunction of these domains contributes to the pathogenesis of Frontotemporal Lobar Degeneration (FTLD) and Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS).

---

## Domain Summary

| Domain | Approximate Residues | Primary Function |
|---------|---------------------:|------------------|
| N-terminal domain (NTD) | 1–102 | Homodimerization and structural stability |
| RRM1 | 106–176 | High-affinity RNA/DNA binding |
| RRM2 | 191–262 | Additional RNA binding and specificity |
| C-terminal glycine-rich domain | 274–414 | Protein interactions, phase separation, aggregation |

---

## Functional Regions

### N-terminal Domain (NTD)

- Mediates TDP-43 self-association (homodimerization)
- Required for normal RNA processing
- Contains the Nuclear Localization Signal (NLS)

### RNA Recognition Motif 1 (RRM1)

- Primary RNA-binding domain
- Recognizes UG-rich RNA sequences
- Essential for splicing regulation

### RNA Recognition Motif 2 (RRM2)

- Stabilizes RNA binding
- Contributes to sequence specificity
- Contains the Nuclear Export Signal (NES)

### C-terminal Domain (CTD)

- Glycine-rich and intrinsically disordered
- Mediates protein-protein interactions
- Critical for stress granule formation
- Major hotspot for ALS and FTLD mutations
- Drives pathological aggregation under disease conditions
